# P1 replay: the jev-pruner at a 2,000-character floor (2026-09-25)

**Verdict: FAIL.** The seed's bar is misses at or under 5% of pruned results AND net tokens saved above zero. Over every
Bash result of 2,000 characters or more in this session (3,153), the pruner pruned nothing, so net tokens saved = 0. It
is also the seed's "Scorer blocked" exit: on the sandbox's local Laya CPU scorer, every one of the 254 results that
reached the scorer failed open. P1 does not go live in the hook. (Task #231, seed `seeds/seed-jev-pipes-p1-v1.yaml`,
brief `tasks/briefs/jev-pipes/P1-brief.md`, D-087.)

## 1. The answer in plain words

- The pruner never pruned. Three things stop it, and each alone is enough:
  1. **Latency.** The local scorer answers one model call per chunk, about 3.8 s each (p50 3,800.9 ms, p90 4,024.4 ms
     per chunk question). The pruner scores at least 3 chunks, so no scored result fits the seed's 2 s budget: the 13
     results that needed a single request all failed open on the budget.
  2. **The queue rule.** The pruner sends one request per history segment, all at once. 241 of the 254 scored results
     need 2 or more requests, so the seed's "queue deeper than one pending request" trip fires at once.
  3. **The scorer's answers.** All 288 answered requests scored every chunk between 0.5163 and 0.5988. The pruner drops a
     chunk only at 0.1 or below (`keepScore`, `vendor/jev-pruner/src/retention.ts:54-56`). For 247 of the 254 scored
     results, the first request held every chunk and every answer was above 0.1, which proves (by the pruner's own keep
     rule, a chunk's score is its best over all segments) that nothing could have been pruned at ANY scorer speed.
- Three structural findings sit under that:
  - **The scorer cannot see the chunk.** The server appends the chunk as the LAST key of the state
    (`dict(base, chunk=chunks[qid])`, `scripts/laya_systemone_server.py:135-139`); Laya keeps the FIRST tokens (its
    `laya/common.py` line 84, `st[:room]` unless `truncate_left`, which `system_one` never sets; `max_len` 1024, of which
    256 hold the question). Across all 294 requests sent, 3,133 to 70,112 characters of state (median 49,926, p10
    27,699) came before the chunk. Within one request the scores differed by at most 0.0069 (p50 0.0010).
  - **The history is longer than the pruner can cover.** After a compaction the window still spans up to 53 history
    segments of 25,000 tokens (median 11). 112 of the 254 scored results need more requests than the hook's cap of 12, so
    even an instant scorer would leave chunks unscored in some segment, and the pruner keeps unscored chunks.
  - **The pruner's own rules leave most results alone.** It never cuts what it reads as a document (a file dump, diff,
    JSON or source text): 2,351 of 3,153 (74.6%). It never scores 2 chunks or fewer: 524 (16.6%). Only 254 (8.1%) reach
    the scorer.

- **The counterfactual with no budget** (`--mode unbudgeted`: no budget or queue trip, requests one at a time, each

  every chunk: `kept_all` 3, `incomplete_coverage` 1 (it used its 12 requests with segments left). The other 56:
  `document` 50, `few_chunks` 6. Pruned 0, net 0.

## 6. Evidence behind the findings, and where it is weak

- Latency, measured three ways: a synthetic 4-chunk request built by the pruner, 15,164 ms; the test fixture's
  recorded requests, 3.7-3.9 s per question (`tests/fixtures/jev_pipes/answers.meta.json`, 9 requests); the full set,
  p50 3,800.9 ms per question.
- Scores with NO history (the chunk inside the window), for synthetic noise, blank lines and dot rows: 0.526-0.552. The
  one real request whose chunk may have been partly visible (3,133 characters before it) scored 0.5494-0.5502. The model
  answers near 0.55 whatever the chunk holds.
- **The shared scorer was contended.** It served 318 requests during the full run, 294 of them this run's, so about 24
  came from other clients; another lane's processes also held about 1.6 of the 4 cores for a while. 6 of the 294 requests sent waited more than 300 s and were cut by
  the HTTP client's default header limit (`UND_ERR_HEADERS_TIMEOUT`); their latency is censored at 300 s and they are
  not in the latency percentiles. The longest answered request took 228,586.5 ms. The verdict does not depend on these:
  all 6 had already failed open on the queue rule.
- Tests: `tests/test_jev_pipes_replay.py` (deterministic, LLM-free; scorer answers recorded from the real server).
  Fail-open is proven for each of the six trips through the vendored pruner and the bridge process, each returning the
  original text unchanged; the floor option keeps the upstream default (a 9,999-token output passes untouched without it).

## 7. NOT run, and limits

- **No miss was measurable.** Nothing was pruned, so the miss test (tokens in the next 20 tool inputs or assistant texts;
  re-runs within 20 calls) ran on no real case. It is tested on the fixture only.
- **The independent verify round (AC7, P1-5, task #260) has not run**; no gate recommendation is recorded yet.
- The live abort path (`live` mode, abort at the budget) is tested against local servers only.
- Modelling choices, stated and not measured: how the runtime joins text blocks (a newline), one history entry per
  assistant `message.id`, `isMeta` messages kept, attachments not messages, and the history used by the miss test (the
  window since the last compaction; the system prompt is not in the transcript). Each choice that adds history makes the
  queue and coverage limits worse, not better; none can raise a score above the keep line or lower one below it.
- No GPU scorer and no PC venue (the seed forbids a GPU scorer beside vLLM; the PC budget would be 5 s).

## 8. What would change the answer (options, not decisions)

- A scorer whose answers separate needed from disposable chunks at the pruner's 0.1 line, and that sees the chunk: the
  state order or the truncation side has to change (the chunk first, or `truncate_left`), or a trained student.
- Far fewer requests per result: the pruner cuts the history into 25,000-token segments and scores every chunk in every
  segment; the seed's queue rule refuses the second request, and the hook's cap of 12 stops coverage.
- A faster scorer: 3 or more chunk questions must fit 2 s in the sandbox (5 s on the PC).
- A wider scope: three quarters of the candidates are documents the pruner never cuts by design.

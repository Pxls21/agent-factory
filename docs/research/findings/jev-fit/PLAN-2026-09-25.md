# JEV-FIT: where Jevs fit our setup (the integration plan, 2026-09-25)

**Status: a design, not a build. No Jev is wired into anything yet.** The owner asked for "actually auditing the system and looking
where how best to fit jev into our setup" (task #256; brief `tasks/briefs/jev-laya/JEV-FIT-brief.md`). Three read-only auditors read
the real code at origin 934bc8a and counted our records, one area each: the coordinator session (A), the build machinery (B), and
the agent factory itself (C). Their reports are in this directory (`area-A.md`, `area-B.md`, `area-C.md`); section 9 lists the
seams the coordinator re-read before writing this plan. The owner's interview follows the plan.

## 1. The answer in plain words

- **A Jev answers one fixed question fast**, with probabilities: yes or no, pick one, or rank a list. It writes no text. It never
  decides a gate. It warns, ranks, points or stops a waste. If it is slow, down or unsure, nothing changes.
- **Most of today's measured waste needs a plain rule, not a model.** Of the fifteen best points (five per area), eight start
  as plain rules; two of them (the lane watch and recall order) hand over to a Jev later. A rule is exact and cheap, and it
  can land this week.
- **A Jev earns its place where a rule cannot see: a question that needs the whole stream of a session.** "Is this lane stuck?",
  "What will the coordinator need after a compaction?", "Which turns hold a lesson?", "Which anti-pattern does this change risk?".
  These are the RWKV stream reader's jobs, plus ranking jobs for Laya.
- **The rules come first, and they make the training data.** Each time a rule fires, we log what it saw and what happened next.
  That log is a labeled example for the Jev that later takes the cases the rule misses. This keeps D-084's "3 birds, 1 stone":
  better tools now, better data, and the factory reuses the same seams.
- **Nothing is proven yet.** No Jev has beaten its rule on held-out data. The Qwen teacher is the first model to beat a rule on one
  question: ranking anti-pattern rows, top-1 0.65 to 0.67 against the lexical rule's 0.59 (QJ2, task #241). Every Jev below ships
  only after it beats the rule named for it.

## 2. Where the waste is (measured)

### A. The coordinator session (area A, sections 1 and 3)

- **The cost is the number of requests.** Each request re-sends the whole context. The main session made 17,241 requests with a
  median context of 460,526 tokens; 7.75 billion of its context tokens were cache reads.
- **The project's hooks add only about 1% of the context**, so a Jev that trims what a hook injects saves at most about 1%.
- **Wasted requests, 2026-09-02 to 09-25:**
  - 1,110 extra turns after a Stop-hook block (602M context tokens). 219 of the 276 blocks came from the harness's git check alone,
    and the extra turn rarely acts: 120 of those 219 turns are text only, and 20 commit.
  - 1,405 requests re-orienting after a compaction (246M): a median of 12 calls per compaction before the first productive call.
  - 551 requests that only touch the task list (271M), and 212 that only load deferred tools (82M).
- **A live bug (checked by the coordinator):** `scripts/orient.sh:27` reads `/root/.claude/projects/-home-user-agent-factory/`, a
  second session's directory last written 2026-09-22. This session's transcript lives in `-home-user/`. So every orientation at
  session start shows 2026-09-22 chat and is cut before the commits section.
- **A correction to JEV-LEVERAGE:** task reminders are 43% of the transcript's bytes, but the harness renders only each task's
  subject. Since the 2026-09-24 cleanup they are 2.4% of context. Nothing more to gain there.

### B. The build machinery (area B, sections 1 and 2)

- **KV-cache starvation:** 4 starved local lanes; vLLM logged 76 preemptions at KV 0.93. The 10-minute heartbeat cannot see an 80 to
  100 s wait.
- **Stuck or looping lanes:** about 3.8 recorded lane-hours (N5k 47 minutes; its xhigh arm 60+ minutes; J1-1-R3 about 2 hours, 80
  calls, 0 edits). At risk: 22 of 71 build lanes and 10 of 48 verify lanes ran 60+ calls with no edit.
- **Oversized lane context:** 286 `skill_view` calls returned 4.31M characters in 45 of 108 lanes, plus many reads of 20k characters
  or more. Big contexts are what fill the KV cache.
- **Poll time-outs:** 34 lanes outlived the dispatcher's 60-minute poll; local lanes run a median 111 minutes (p90 280).
- **The costliest loop is the verify round:** 57 NOT-READY of 93 recommendations.

### C. The agent factory (area C, sections 1 and 2)

- Stages 2, 5 and 6 are not built, so every product payoff is unmeasured. The seats are clear, and so is the fence: never in the
  fail-closed `pre_tool_call` policy hook, the governance core or the evaluators. A rule is enough there, and a Jev would break
  fail-open.

## 4. Shared infrastructure (build once; it serves all three areas)

1. **One stream.** SESSION-EXPORT's event schema (D-1) for every source: Claude Code transcripts (a tailer), Hermes lanes (the fact
   recorder), and later the product (a Hermes plugin). Text is scrubbed at the source, before any byte leaves.
2. **One label log.** Every hook and rule decision is written with a pointer into the stream (the hook payload's `transcript_path`
   and a byte offset), which makes each decision a labeled example (D-086). Durable rows go to the decision ledger
   (`src/agent_factory/decisions/ledger.py`), which needs new source kinds (`_VALID_KINDS` at `:46-52` has none for hook or lane
   events) and a row form for a model's answer.
3. **One stream-state server** on the PC (RWKV). Not built; its CPU speed and GPU room are Phase 2's first measurements.
4. **One return path.** For this session: an advice file the hooks read in microseconds. For Hermes lanes and the product: the spool
   that `post_tool_call` writes and `pre_llm_call` drains (`harness-ports/hermes/config-snippet.yaml:137-139`, 30 s budget). Stale
   or missing advice changes nothing.
5. **The fence.** KC-J1's never-a-gate screen walks `scripts/hooks/*`, the workflows and the proof checkers
   (`scripts/no_laya_in_gates.py:1268-1285`); `scripts/gate_files.txt` names no `src/` file. Extend it to the product's gate code
   before any product Jev.

## 5. What it saves and improves, honestly

- **Tokens (the coordinator):** R1 and R2 target about 895 requests and 291M context tokens; R7 targets 763 more requests. These
  are ceilings over 23 days, mostly cache reads. After each rule lands we measure the real share: calls per compaction before the
  first productive call (12 today), and requests per git-only Stop block (2.7 today).
- **GPU hours and wall time (the PC):** R3 and R6 target the starved and stuck lanes; R4 shrinks lane contexts so more lanes fit.
- **Accuracy and quality:** anti-pattern warnings before a commit; the verify lane attacks the likeliest rows first; a looping lane
  is caught in minutes, not hours. None of these is measured yet.
- **What a Jev never does:** decide a gate, block a tool on its own, or write code. The deterministic gates and the owner decide.

## 6. How each Jev is judged (kill criteria)

- Trained on our records, tested on held-out examples, compared with its named rule. If it does not beat the rule, the rule stays
  and the Jev does not ship.
- Ships advisory-only, and logs its answers beside the rule's before any consumer reads them.
- Its question is asked before the answer appears in the stream (the deep-work label-position rule): 18 of 93 verifier lanes wrote
  their recommendation into a file before their final message.

## 7. Risks the audits named

- **GPU:** RWKV beside vLLM on the 3090 is unmeasured; vLLM holds about 95% of the card (AF-AP-201).
- **A feedback loop:** a model trained on our sessions will shape our sessions. The rule-first order and the held-out comparison
  limit it.
- **Labels record what happened, not always what was right** (82 dispatches omitted the model against a written rule).
- **Privacy:** the stream holds the owner's own messages. Everything is scrubbed at the source, and a student trained on product
  sessions stays with its tenant.
- **Hook weight:** every hook seam adds latency and a failure mode. The design keeps Jevs out of the hot path: hooks read a cached
  answer, and nothing waits on a model.

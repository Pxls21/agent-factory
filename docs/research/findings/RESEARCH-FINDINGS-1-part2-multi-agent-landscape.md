# Maximizing Throughput and Accuracy for a Multi-Concurrent-Agent System on a Local Qwen3-27B-Class Model

## TL;DR
- **The single biggest win for a many-agent "factory" is NOT single-stream speculative decoding — it is cross-agent KV reuse plus continuous batching.** Because your agents share a large stable system prompt + project-context prefix every turn, a serving engine with automatic prefix caching + KV offload (SGLang RadixAttention, or vLLM + LMCache) recovers the ~81% redundant prefill that a single-stream llama.cpp server recomputes, and lets many decode streams share the GPU. But your model is a hybrid Gated-DeltaNet (GDN) architecture, and prefix caching for hybrid Mamba/GDN models is fragile in vLLM today — this is the central technical risk and it dictates the recommended architecture.
- **Keep llama.cpp/lucebox on the single 3090 for latency-sensitive lanes with DFlash; move the multi-agent fan-out to a second, bigger card running SGLang (or vLLM ≥0.28 with `mamba_cache_mode=all`).** On one 24 GB Ampere card the 27B 4-bit barely fits (~18–19 GB weights, leaving only enough KV headroom for ~64K context at fp8/Q8 KV), so true N-way concurrency at long context needs more VRAM. DFlash gives ~2.5–3.4× single-stream decode on the 3090 but was single-stream in the original daemon; concurrency/paged-attention is newer and the speedup *ratio* peaks around 3 concurrent streams.
- **Accuracy-boosting test-time compute (optillm) belongs behind your router as an opt-in lane, not the default** — best-of-N / self-consistency / mixture-of-agents raise coding-and-reasoning accuracy but multiply tokens, which is expensive when N agents already saturate the GPU. Reserve it for the verify/oracle lane and for hard tasks. Every quant, KV-compression, speculative and test-time knob below is tagged with its accuracy risk.

## Key Findings

**1. Your workload is a textbook "coding-agent" serving profile, and the literature now quantifies it.** The TraceLab study (arXiv 2606.30560, ~4,300 sessions / 350K LLM steps / 430K tool calls) found coding agents have "long autonomous loops, long contexts with short outputs, diverse and heavily-tailed tool calls, and high but imperfect prefix cache hit rates." Critically: **95.7% of input tokens hit the prefix cache in aggregate, but only 19% of appended tokens are genuinely new — "every new token is prefilled roughly 5× on average."** This is the single largest inefficiency in your current single-stream setup, and it is exactly what cross-request KV reuse eliminates.

**2. Prefix caching for your hybrid GDN architecture is the crux.** Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8 use the Gated-DeltaNet 3:1 hybrid (≈75% linear/recurrent layers, ≈25% full attention). Recurrent state is not token-position-decomposable, so standard block-level prefix caching silently fails: multiple vLLM issues document **0% prefix-cache hits** on Qwen3.5 hybrids (#40696, #43587, #45238, #51250). vLLM is actively fixing this with `mamba_cache_mode=all` (PRs #36649, #50172), which restores block-level GDN state caching with byte-identical outputs, but it is new. This is the deciding architectural fact.

**3. On a single 24 GB Ampere card the 27B-4bit is VRAM-bound, capping true concurrency.** ~18–19 GB of weights leaves only a few GB for KV, so multi-slot long-context serving is severely limited (~64K ceiling with fp8/Q8 KV). Ampere (sm_86) has no native FP8, so FP8 KV/weight paths dequantize rather than accelerate. Real N-way concurrency at 100K+ context needs a second GPU or a bigger card.

**4. DFlash is the best single-stream accelerator for your exact stack — but it is a decode/prefill latency tool, not a throughput-scaling tool.** DFlash (z-lab, arXiv 2602.06036) block-diffusion drafting is already merged/mergeable in llama.cpp (`--spec-type draft-dflash`), in vLLM (`method: dflash`), and is the core of lucebox. It delivers real gains and is numerically validatable, but its win is largest single-stream and predictable-continuation (code).

## Details

### Per-repo assessments (the six provided)

**1. Luce-Org/lucebox (+ lucebox-hub)** — *Verdict: ADOPT for the single-3090 low-latency lane; do NOT rely on it as the multi-agent fabric.*
A llama.cpp fork (Apache-2.0 on the main repo; ~2.8k stars; effectively a small team / commercial hardware venture led by Davide Ciffa) implementing **DFlash** (block-diffusion speculative decode + DDTree verifier with three custom `ggml_gated_delta_net_tree*` ops) and **PFlash** (speculative prefill). It is purpose-built for exactly your target: Qwen3.x-27B GGUF on a single RTX 3090 (sm_86 reference).
- **DFlash decode on 3090, Qwen3.5-27B Q4_K_M:** HumanEval 37.78→129.52 tok/s (3.43×), Math500 2.93×, GSM8K 2.55×; [GitHub](https://github.com/Luce-Org/lucebox-hub/tree/main/dflash) demo peak 207.6 tok/s (5.46×, best single run). [lucebox](https://www.lucebox.com/blog/dflash27b) 128K context fits in 24 GB via Q4_0 KV: 134.78 tok/s at ctx=131072. [lucebox](https://www.lucebox.com/blog/dflash27b) [GitHub](https://github.com/Luce-Org/lucebox-hub/tree/main) Cross-validated vs PyTorch oracle at cos-sim 0.999812; "bit-identical to autoregressive at 40 tokens" with f16 intermediate cache [lucebox](https://www.lucebox.com/blog/dflash27b) — so decode is numerically safe at greedy.
- **PFlash prefill on 3090, Qwen3.6-27B Q4_K_M:** 128K TTFT 24.8 s vs ~257 s vanilla llama.cpp (~10.4×); 64K 13.5 s vs 134.95 s (10×); NIAH single-needle preserved 32K→128K at keep_ratio=0.05. [GitHub](https://github.com/Luce-Org/lucebox-hub/tree/main/pflash) **But PFlash is explicitly "lossy prompt compression"** — the README says "keep it off for exact-retrieval and matched true-context benchmarks," [GitHub](https://github.com/Luce-Org/lucebox) and independent tester Allen Kuo reproduced the prefill speedup but reported an unresolved decode-quality blocker on the integrated path. **Accuracy risk: HIGH for PFlash; LOW for DFlash decode at greedy.**
- **Concurrency:** original DFlash daemon was concurrency=1 ("greedy decoding, n_gen=256"). [GitHub](https://github.com/Luce-Org/lucebox-hub/blob/main/dflash/RESULTS.md) The current repo adds `--paged-attention --max-concurrency N` (paged KV, "82% less KV memory, 1.35× attention step"), and claims 106.6→300.9 tok/s from 1→5 clients [lucebox](https://www.lucebox.com/blog) — **but that scaling number is on an AMD R9700, not a 3090.** Under concurrency the DFlash speedup *ratio* peaks around 3 streams then declines slightly (per Kuo's vLLM DFlash data: c1 2.29×, c3 2.49×, c4 2.26×).
- Caveats: greedy-only on the older path (temperature/top_p accepted and ignored); DFlash lib hardcoded for one target+draft pair; Q4 quantization "costs ~30 points of per-position accept vs BF16," [lucebox](https://www.lucebox.com/blog/dflash27b) and rejection sampling across the DDTree is incomplete (samples only the committed token). Many headline numbers (208/227 tok/s, 6.4×) are R9700, not 3090.

**2. leoheuler/flashtensors** — *Verdict: ADOPT if the factory runs multiple model roles / hot-swaps; not needed for a single always-resident model.*
Fast model loader (built substantially on ServerlessLLM code) that streams weights SSD→GPU "up to 10× faster than safetensors," hot-swaps in <2 s, [GitHub](https://github.com/leoheuler/flashtensors) hosts "100+ models" [X](https://x.com/unwind_ai_/status/1987743940527931759) via a daemon + gRPC, integrates with vLLM. On H100 it loads a 32B in <5 s (4–6× vs standard loaders). [Medium](https://thamizhelango.medium.com/flashtensors-a-deep-dive-into-ultra-fast-model-loading-for-llm-inference-5f9ba0023e3b) Relevant only if you have distinct model roles (drafter, verifier, planner, coder) that must swap on one card. On a single 3090 that already holds one 27B resident, there's no room to hot-swap a second 27B — so this is a "second GPU / bigger card" tool, or for swapping small auxiliary models (drafts, embedders). **Accuracy risk: NONE (loading only).** Alternatives: ServerlessLLM itself (6–10× faster loading, GPU multiplexing, "serve 10 models on 1 GPU"), vLLM sleep/wake mode (offload to CPU, reload without full reinit), fastsafetensors/FlashPack (3–6× load).

**3. Mega4alik/ollm** — *Verdict: NOT a fit for interactive multi-agent serving; keep as an offline escape hatch for oversized-context single tasks.*
Lightweight HF-Transformers/PyTorch engine that streams weights and KV to NVMe (via KvikIO/cuFile), no quantization, targeting 100K context on 8 GB GPUs. [X](https://x.com/Marktechpost/status/1972720815839052267) But throughput is offline-grade: the 80B MoE example runs ~0.5 tok/s on an RTX 3060 Ti [X](https://x.com/Marktechpost/status/1972720815839052267) ("1 token every 2 seconds"), and it uses DiskCache KV offload (52.4 GB for Llama-3-8B at 100K). [Medium](https://medium.com/data-science-in-your-pocket/revolutionizing-large-context-llm-inference-a-deep-dive-into-the-ollm-python-library-aacda4928a6f) No continuous batching, no multi-slot serving. It solves "fit a huge context/model on tiny VRAM at any speed," which is the opposite of your decode-bound, latency-sensitive many-agent need. **Accuracy risk: LOW (fp16/bf16, no quant) but irrelevant at 0.5 tok/s.**

**4. MaximeRivest/ovllm** — *Verdict: OPTIONAL convenience wrapper; not infrastructure.*
A ~500-LOC single-file vLLM wrapper with DSPy integration and auto-batching (groups concurrent requests into one vLLM batch). [GitHub](https://github.com/MaximeRivest/ovllm) Overhead is negligible (65 s vLLM vs 68 s via ovllm on the author's test). [X](https://x.com/MaximeRivest/status/1952503111718694965) It is an ergonomics layer for DSPy pipelines, not a serving or optimization engine — if you adopt vLLM and drive it from Python/DSPy it's a nicety, but your existing OmniRoute router already provides the OpenAI-compatible surface. **Accuracy risk: NONE.**

**5. algorithmicsuperintelligence/optillm** — *Verdict: ADOPT as an opt-in accuracy lane behind the router; never as the default path for all agents.*
OpenAI-compatible optimizing proxy that per its GitHub README "implements 20+ state-of-the-art techniques" (best-of-N, mixture-of-agents `moa-`, CoT-decoding, self-consistency, PlanSearch, re-reading, MCTS, CePO, self-discover, entropy decoding). It sits in front of any OpenAI-compatible endpoint [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/optillm/) — so it can front your local llama.cpp/vLLM/SGLang endpoint behind OmniRoute. Published gains: the project reports "We saw huge performance gains across all the supported patchflows...when using the mixture of agents approach (moa)" via the patchwork PR-review/bugfix/security framework (self-consistency default k=6); it supports local decoding techniques (cot_decoding, entropy_decoding) that require logprobs. **The cost is tokens: MoA/best-of-N/self-consistency multiply generation several-fold**, which directly competes with your N concurrent agents for GPU. Fit: route only *hard* tasks or the *verify/oracle* step through optillm; keep the main agent loop single-pass. **Accuracy risk: POSITIVE (raises accuracy) but changes outputs — validate against your gates, since best-of-N with a weak verifier can select confidently-wrong answers.**

**6. LMCache/LMCache** — *Verdict: ADOPT on the vLLM path for cross-agent KV reuse + CPU/NVMe offload — but its benefit on hybrid GDN models is gated by vLLM's hybrid prefix-caching support.*
A KV-cache layer for vLLM: prefix reuse across requests/instances, KV offload to CPU RAM / local disk, disaggregated prefill [Lmcache](https://lmcache.ai/tech_report.pdf) (NIXL transport), and cross-instance KV sharing. Per the LMCache tech report (Yao/Jiang et al., arXiv 2510.09665, University of Chicago), "combining LMCACHE with vLLM achieves up to 15x improvement in throughput across workloads such as multi-round question answering and document analysis" — but that 15× is the high-concurrency (~200 req), ~80%-reuse upper bound; smaller production workloads report 4–5× TTFT improvement. It achieves 400 Gbps CPU-offload load bandwidth vs 88 Gbps for vLLM's native offload, and reduces mean TTFT 1.53–1.84× / ITL 1.12–1.66× vs vLLM native PD-disagg. vLLM's own KV-offloading connector (v0.11.0, blog dated 2026-01-08) shows "System throughput can increase by up to 9x as the cache hit rate in CPU memory increases," with "2–22x faster time-to-first-token for cached prompts." For your case the value is enormous *if* the shared prefix's KV can be reused — but on hybrid GDN the recurrent state must be reconstructed at the shared boundary, so LMCache's prefix reuse only helps the ~25% full-attention layers unless `mamba_cache_mode=all` (GDN state caching) is enabled. LMCache shines most across *multiple* vLLM instances / a second GPU (disaggregated prefill needs ≥2 GPUs to separate P and D). On one 24 GB card its main use is CPU KV offload to survive preemption and grow the effective cache. **Accuracy risk: LOW (KV reuse is lossless if state is reconstructed correctly; the hybrid-state replay is the correctness-sensitive part).**

### Landscape by direction, ranked by impact for a MULTI-AGENT deployment

**A. Cross-agent KV reuse & prefix caching — HIGHEST IMPACT.**
The winner for N agents sharing a long stable prefix is automatic prefix caching + a radix/tree cache + KV offload. **SGLang RadixAttention** is the most mature: it stores KV in a radix tree and reuses across requests automatically. The peer-reviewed SGLang paper (Zheng et al., arXiv 2312.07104, NeurIPS'24) reports "SGLang achieves up to 6.4× higher throughput compared to state-of-the-art inference systems...on tasks including agent control, logical reasoning, few-shot learning" (the earlier LMSYS blog cited 5×), and the LMSYS ablation found "no noticeable overhead even in the absence of cache hits, leading us to always enable the RadixAttention feature in the runtime." **vLLM APC + LMCache** is the alternative with better CPU/NVMe offload and cross-instance sharing. **CacheBlend/CacheGen** extend reuse to non-prefix chunks (RAG). **KVFlow** (Pan et al., arXiv 2507.07400, NeurIPS 2025, built on SGLang v0.4.4) targets multi-agent workflows specifically, reporting "up to 1.83× speedup for single workflows with large prompts, and up to 2.19× speedup for scenarios with many concurrent workflows" vs SGLang's hierarchical radix cache. **The caveat that dominates everything: for Qwen3.5-class GDN hybrids, all of these need the engine to cache the recurrent GDN state, not just attention KV** — otherwise hits drop to ~0% (vLLM issues #40696/#45238/#51250; the "HOT continuation" RFC #56768 is a hybrid-specific single-session workaround). *Accuracy risk: LOW.*

**B. Speculative decoding beyond MTP — HIGH single-stream, MODERATE at scale.**
- **DFlash** (block-diffusion, z-lab arXiv 2602.06036): 5-layer draft (8 for Qwen3-Coder), block size 16, trained DFlash/DFlash2 drafts exist for Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8-27B (e.g. `z-lab/Qwen3.5-9B-DFlash`, [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22105) Lucebox Q8_0 GGUF drafts). Supported in llama.cpp (`draft-dflash`), vLLM (`method:dflash`), MLX. Claims up to 8× (Qwen3), [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06036) [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22105) beats EAGLE-3 by +15% acceptance. On your 3090 GGUF: 2.5–3.4× decode. **This is the best speculative fit for your exact model.** *Accuracy risk: LOW at greedy (numerically validated); acceptance/quality drops with Q4 draft softmax flattening.*
- **EAGLE-2/EAGLE-3**: SOTA autoregressive draft-head speculative, 2–2.5× in vLLM, widely deployed (vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT-LLM). Trained heads exist for many models; **P-EAGLE** (vLLM blog, Mar 2026) adds parallel drafting. Needs a trained head for Qwen3.x-27B specifically; works under continuous batching. *Accuracy risk: LOW (lossless verification).*
- **MTP** (your current): built into Qwen3.5/3.8; ~1.9× single-stream but "does not stack with parallel slots" — consistent with the finding that MTP net-wins require concurrency≥4 AND accept≥80% to amortize the verify pass.
- **ngram/prompt-lookup** (llama.cpp `ngram-*`, vLLM suffix/ngram): free, no draft model, good for repetitive code/tool-output continuation; stacks with continuous batching. *Accuracy risk: NONE (lossless).* Recommended as an always-on cheap layer.
- **Medusa/Hydra/lookahead/self-speculative (layer-skip)**: older or lower-acceptance; superseded by EAGLE-3/DFlash for this model class.
- **Cross-engine verdict:** For speculative + continuous batching on the GDN hybrid in 2026, **vLLM and SGLang** have the most complete combination (both support EAGLE3 + DFlash + MTP under continuous batching; note PD-separation constrains `num_speculative_tokens` equality on hybrid Mamba models). [vLLM](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/ascend/en/main/user_guide/feature_guide/speculative_decoding.html) **ik_llama.cpp** brings fused delta-net (GDN), MTP for Qwen3.5/3.6, DFlash, and SOTA quants to the llama.cpp world with often-better performance. [GitHub](https://github.com/yoirtbryggffdcfgcbcbcffdbfxx/ik_llama.cpp)

**C. Higher-throughput serving engines — DECISION POINT.**
- **vLLM ≥0.28**: continuous batching, PagedAttention, APC, chunked prefill, MTP for Qwen3.5, speculative (EAGLE/DFlash/ngram), LMCache integration, sleep/wake, KV offload connector. Supports Qwen3.5 dense+MoE. **GGUF is being deprecated in-tree** (moved to an out-of-tree `vllm-gguf-plugin`); the native fast path on Ampere is **AWQ/GPTQ+Marlin**.
- **SGLang**: RadixAttention (best automatic prefix sharing), structured output, EAGLE/MTP, merged Gated-DeltaNet linear-attention layer for Qwen3.5 (PR #1059). [GitHub](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang-jax/issues/1251) AWQ runs on SGLang; GGUF for this arch was reported broken as of 2026-04 [GitHub](https://github.com/Luce-Org/lucebox-hub/tree/main/dflash) per lucebox.
- **TensorRT-LLM**: in-flight batching, best raw throughput on Hopper/Blackwell, but its headline advantage is FP8 — **useless on Ampere (no FP8)** and heavier to operate. Not recommended for a 3090.
- **Concrete keep-vs-switch:** Keep **llama.cpp/lucebox+DFlash on the 3090** for the latency-critical lane. For the multi-agent fan-out, **switch to SGLang** (best prefix sharing for your shared-prefix workload) **or vLLM** (if you need LMCache offload + broader ecosystem), running an **AWQ 4-bit** Qwen3.x-27B rather than GGUF. On one 24 GB card expect only a handful of concurrent long-context agents; realistic aggregate throughput scales with a second card. On a 48 GB card (or 2×3090) you can host the 27B with real KV headroom and serve ~8–16 concurrent agents with aggregate decode in the hundreds of tok/s under continuous batching.

**D. Fast model loading / hot-swap — SITUATIONAL.**
flashtensors / ServerlessLLM / vLLM sleep-wake matter only if the factory swaps model roles. If you standardize on one resident 27B + small drafts, this is low priority. If you run planner/coder/verifier as distinct models, ServerlessLLM's GPU multiplexing ("10 models on 1 GPU," 6–10× faster load) [GitHub](https://github.com/ServerlessLLM/ServerlessLLM) or flashtensors (<2 s hot-swap) [GitHub](https://github.com/leoheuler/flashtensors) is the enabling tech — but VRAM, not load time, is the binding constraint on 24 GB. *Accuracy risk: NONE.*

**E. Quantization & kernels for Ampere — MODERATE.**
- **GGUF IQ4_XS/Q4_K_M** (current): best-supported for GDN in llama.cpp/ik_llama.cpp; good quality; but slow in vLLM.
- **AWQ+Marlin**: the fastest 4-bit decode path on Ampere in vLLM. Per JarvisLabs' vLLM quantization benchmark, un-Marlin AWQ ran 67 tok/s and Marlin-AWQ hit "741 tok/s - a 10.9x speedup," versus GGUF (Q4_K_M) at 93 tok/s (TTFT 958 ms); all methods stayed within ~6% perplexity of baseline and all scored 51.8% HumanEval Pass@1, and GPTQ's Marlin speedup was 2.6×. **This is the recommended quant if you move to vLLM/SGLang.**
- **GPTQ+Marlin**: similar (2.6× Marlin speedup); AWQ generally edges it on quality.
- **ExLlamaV2/EXL2/EXL3**: excellent quality-per-bit and fast on Ampere, but weaker multi-agent continuous-batching story than vLLM/SGLang.
- **ik_llama.cpp**: fused GDN kernels, MTP for Qwen3.5/3.6, DFlash, SOTA quants [GitHub](https://github.com/yoirtbryggffdcfgcbcbcffdbfxx/ik_llama.cpp) (IQ*_KS, trellis), often faster than mainline llama.cpp — worth benchmarking as a drop-in for the 3090 lane.
- Ampere has no FP8; avoid FP8 weight/KV paths (they dequantize). *Accuracy risk: MODERATE — any quant change must be re-validated against your gates; AWQ/GPTQ and GGUF Q4 are all "practical" but not identical to bf16.*

**F. Accuracy-boosting test-time compute — POSITIVE accuracy, HIGH token cost.**
optillm's MoA/best-of-N/self-consistency/PlanSearch raise coding/reasoning accuracy but multiply tokens. In a many-agent system where the GPU is already saturated, applying these to every agent turn is self-defeating. **Best fit: (a) the adversarial verify/oracle lane** (where extra compute buys grading reliability), **and (b) a "hard task" escalation route** the router selects for. Self-consistency and best-of-N pair naturally with your existing verifier oracle — the verifier is the selector, which mitigates the "confidently-wrong selection" failure mode of unverified best-of-N. *Accuracy risk: POSITIVE if the verifier is strong; NEGATIVE if best-of-N is selected by log-prob alone.*

**G. Long-context efficiency — MODERATE, use with care.**
- **KV quantization (Q8_0/Q4_0 KV in llama.cpp; KIVI)**: Q8 KV is near-lossless and the recommended default; Q4 KV costs a few % (lucebox: "~3% quality on HE" at short context, worse long). [lucebox](https://www.lucebox.com/blog/dflash27b) *Accuracy risk: LOW at Q8, MODERATE at Q4.*
- **Token-eviction compression (SnapKV, PyramidKV, H2O)**: SnapKV keeps avg drops <1.5% at 10% retention on LongBench, [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01068) but degrades needle-retrieval beyond ~6K context (SnapKV NIAH 57.3 vs FullKV 65.0). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02069v1) PyramidKV preserves retrieval better (62.6). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02069v1) **For agents doing exact file/test retrieval, aggressive eviction is dangerous.** *Accuracy risk: HIGH for retrieval-critical agent turns.*
- **SSD/CPU KV offload (oLLM-style, LMCache disk)**: grows capacity, not speed; use to survive, not to accelerate.
- **PFlash speculative prefill**: 10× TTFT but lossy — same caution as SnapKV.
- **Context management (harness-side)**: TraceLab shows human/idle gaps cause cache eviction; keeping the KV alive across gaps and refreshing it, plus append-length-aware prefill, cuts redundant prefill without touching quality. **This is the safest long-context win.**

**H. Scheduling & orchestration — HIGH IMPACT, under-exploited.**
TraceLab's findings translate directly: (1) route follow-up turns of an agent back to the instance/GPU that already holds its KV (cache-affinity routing — Preble, llm-d); (2) use prefix-aware scheduling so agents sharing the system prompt batch together (RadixAttention does this automatically); (3) admission control / fair-share so one long-context agent doesn't starve others; (4) prefill/decode separation on multi-GPU (LMCache/NIXL) so a big prefill doesn't stall everyone's decode; (5) predictive KV eviction from tool-call metadata (CacheWise). Your OmniRoute router should implement cache-affinity + prefix-aware batching + priority lanes (interactive vs background agents). *Accuracy risk: NONE (scheduling only).*

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Instrument before changing anything (this week).** Measure per-agent prefix-cache hit rate, TTFT, decode tok/s at your real 100K–260K contexts, and aggregate tok/s at N=2/4/8 concurrent agents on the current llama.cpp server. Decision rule: if aggregate throughput is flat or falls as N rises (it will, on one stream-oriented server), the bottleneck is serving architecture, not the model.

**Stage 1 — Single-3090 version (low risk, keep current paradigm):**
1. Adopt **ik_llama.cpp or lucebox DFlash** for the 3090 lane: DFlash draft for Qwen3.8-27B, `--spec-type draft-dflash`, Q8_0 KV, greedy for max acceptance. Expect ~2.5–3.4× decode on code-heavy turns. Keep **ngram/prompt-lookup** on as a free always-on layer.
2. Turn on **paged-attention + `--max-concurrency`** (lucebox) or continuous batching to serve a *small* number of agents; don't expect the DFlash ratio to survive past ~3 concurrent streams.
3. Add **optillm behind OmniRoute as an opt-in lane** for the verify/oracle step and hard-task escalation only.
4. Keep KV at **Q8_0**; do NOT use PFlash or SnapKV on retrieval-critical turns.
- Benchmark that flips to Stage 2: if you need >3–4 concurrent long-context agents, or aggregate tok/s is the binding constraint.

**Stage 2 — Add a second/bigger GPU for the multi-agent fabric (the real fix):**
1. Stand up **SGLang** (first choice, for RadixAttention automatic prefix sharing) or **vLLM ≥0.28 + LMCache** on a 48 GB card (or 2×3090), serving **AWQ 4-bit** Qwen3.x-27B (not GGUF).
2. **Enable hybrid GDN prefix caching** (`mamba_cache_mode=all` on vLLM, or SGLang's GDN path) and verify hit-rate >0 with a shared-prefix probe — this is the make-or-break test for your architecture. If hits stay ~0%, fall back to session-affinity routing (route each agent's turns to the instance holding its recurrent state; see RFC #56768).
3. Add **EAGLE-3 or DFlash under continuous batching** for per-stream speedup that coexists with batching.
4. Use **LMCache CPU/NVMe offload** to hold more agents' KV than VRAM allows, and **prefill/decode disaggregation** across the two GPUs so large prefills don't stall decode.
5. Router: **cache-affinity + prefix-aware batching + priority lanes.**
- Projection: on a 48 GB card with continuous batching + prefix reuse, expect to serve roughly 8–16 concurrent agents with aggregate decode in the low-hundreds of tok/s, versus a single stream today — the gain comes mostly from eliminated redundant prefill (the ~5× re-prefill tax) and batched decode, not from raw per-token speed.

**Stage 3 — Multi-model factory (only if roles diverge):** add **ServerlessLLM or flashtensors** for fast role-swap, and **vLLM sleep/wake** to park idle models.

## Caveats
- **Accuracy is the hard constraint, and several high-throughput knobs move outputs.** DFlash at greedy is numerically validated (cos-sim 0.9998, bit-identical at short gen) — LOW risk. But **PFlash prompt compression, SnapKV/H2O eviction, and Q4 KV are lossy** and dangerous for exact retrieval / test-gate grading — validate each against your adversarial oracle before enabling. Quant changes (GGUF→AWQ) must be re-graded.
- **The hybrid GDN prefix-caching support in vLLM is new and has documented failure modes (0% hits).** Do not assume cross-agent KV reuse "just works" on Qwen3.5-class models — probe it. This is the single largest risk to the recommended architecture, and the reason SGLang (with a merged GDN layer) and session-affinity routing are the safer bets.
- **Many of lucebox's most impressive numbers are on non-3090 hardware (AMD R9700, RTX 5090).** The validated single-3090 Qwen-27B figures are ~130 tok/s mean HumanEval DFlash decode and ~10× PFlash TTFT (lossy). Treat the 207/227 tok/s and 300 tok/s concurrency figures as other-hardware or best-case demos.
- **TensorRT-LLM's FP8 advantage does not apply on Ampere.** Don't invest there unless you move to Hopper/Blackwell.
- **optillm and multi-agent test-time compute multiply token cost**, directly competing with concurrency. They raise accuracy per task but lower aggregate agent throughput — use selectively.
- **Security:** all recommended engines (llama.cpp, vLLM, SGLang) serve on loopback and fit your single-router-as-sole-egress model; LMCache disaggregation adds a NIXL transport between workers that must stay on the trusted host/network.

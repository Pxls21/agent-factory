# Blazingly-Fast Local Build/Verify Lanes on One RTX 3090: A Ranked Recipe for Qwen3.8-27B + llama.cpp + Hermes

## TL;DR
- **Stay on llama.cpp.** The single biggest wins are not more speculative decoding — they are (1) cutting OUTPUT tokens by dropping Qwen3.8-27B's default `reasoning_effort` from `xhigh` to `medium` (~1.8x fewer decode tokens), (2) fixing a self-inflicted ~30–37% long-context decode penalty from your `q4_0` KV cache by moving K/V to `q8_0`/`f16` (which is also *higher* quality, not lower), and (3) trimming Hermes input tokens per tool call. Together these attack the 80%-decode / 20%-prefill split directly and are largely accuracy-neutral or accuracy-positive.
- **The two-lanes-in-parallel goal forces a fork you have not measured.** MTP gives ~1.85–1.90x single-stream but does **not** stack with parallel slots; the non-MTP path *does* scale aggregate throughput with `-np`. On one 3090 you can get ~1.9x from MTP-single-stream **or** ~1.5–2.5x aggregate from `-np 2–3` continuous batching, but **not both multiplied**. For "2+ lanes concurrently at 2–3h each," the evidence points to **dropping MTP and running `-np 2` continuous batching** — but this exact cell (aggregate decode under 2–3 concurrent 100K+ lane-shaped requests) was never benchmarked and must be measured first.
- **Projected outcome on the recommended config:** a build lane drops from ~4h53m–6h41m (10h reported) to **~2.5–3.5h**, with **2 lanes running concurrently** (both finishing in that window), at equal-or-better accuracy. A verify lane, which is shorter-output and more prefill-heavy, projects to **~1–2h**. Switching engines (vLLM/SGLang) is **not** recommended: the 27B hybrid barely fits 4-bit on 24 GB and its long-context + MTP + continuous-batching paths are buggy today.

## Key Findings

1. **Your workload is decode-bound, so the highest-leverage knob is fewer output tokens.** Qwen3.8-27B ships with `reasoning_effort` defaulted to `xhigh`, the most expensive of three levels (`xhigh`/`medium`/`low`), and it is notorious for burning tens of thousands of reasoning tokens with no accuracy gain (a trivial "draw an SVG circle" request famously consumed 22,000+ reasoning tokens and ~21 minutes at the default). [Luong Hong Thuan](https://luonghongthuan.com/en/blog/qwen3-8-27b-reasoning-effort-trap/) Your own note that `xhigh` ≈ 1.8x the output tokens "for no better landing" matches the public record. Moving to `medium` is the single largest wall-clock lever and is gated only by an A/B on the verify-lane oracle.

2. **Your `q4_0` KV cache is silently costing ~30–37% of decode speed at long context.** A controlled llama.cpp benchmark (Discussion #20969, TurboQuant thread — contributor dentity007's corrected DGX Spark GB10 run, build 8399, Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B Q4_K_XL, 128K ctx) shows generation throughput with `q4_0` KV degrades from **+0.7% at ~6K (f16 44.7 / q4_0 45.0), to −11.9% at ~24K (44.6 / 39.3), to −36.8% at ~110K (38.0 / 24.0)** "due to per-token dequantization overhead" — while prefill is unaffected. [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/20969) Because Qwen3.8-27B is a hybrid where **only 16 of 65 layers use a KV cache** (per froggeric/Qwen3.6-27B-MTP-GGUF: "The other 48 are linear attention (fixed 150 MiB recurrent state). KV memory is ~4× less than a standard dense model"), [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/froggeric/Qwen3.6-27B-MTP-GGUF) your KV footprint is far smaller than a normal 27B, leaving room to move K/V to `q8_0` or `f16`. This is a rare **faster-AND-higher-quality** lever.

3. **MTP does not compose with parallel slots.** llama.cpp PR #22673 (the MTP implementation) states verbatim that "Parallel decoding with MTP is supported, but not fully optimized yet," [github](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) and lists as an open TODO: "Improve multi-seq performance of the recurrent memory for `n_rs_seq > 0` (currently the multi-seq states are not contiguous [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) in memory so cannot be batched together)." [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) [github](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) Community testing on a 3090-class box shows MTP delivers ~1.9x at concurrency 1 but goes flat as concurrency rises, whereas the **non-MTP path scales aggregate throughput up with concurrency**. This is the central architectural tradeoff for your parallel-lanes goal.

4. **A separate small draft model is a net loss on this pairing.** Multiple 3090 reports find Qwen3.5-0.8B/Qwen3-0.6B as `--model-draft` for a 27B *regresses* throughput vs baseline (on 2×3090, Qwen3.6-27B dropped from ~53 tps to 33 tps, and to 18 tps with ngram-mod added), [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/22473) and every llama.cpp speculative mode tested on the 35B sibling on a 3090 was slower than baseline. MTP (built into the GGUF) is the better speculative option; do not add an external draft model.

5. **vLLM/SGLang do not rescue you on a single 3090.** The 27B hybrid AWQ-INT4 fits ~18.8 GiB of weights on 24 GB, leaving only ~2.1 GiB of KV → **~64K context ceiling** with fp8 KV (verified datapoint: github.com/dkhokhlov/Qwen_3_6_on_A10_vLLM on a 24 GB A10); reaching 100K+ needs out-of-tree TurboQuant 3-bit-KV + MTP patches (one enthusiast hit ~85 tps @ 125K on a single 3090 that way, single-stream only) or a second GPU. MTP under vLLM continuous batching currently caps at ~3 concurrent sequences on this architecture (open bug #55533; MTP becomes 2–3x *slower* at batch≥4) and has prefix-cache recompute penalties (#53670, fixed only via an opt-in flag, not default). **Recommendation: keep llama.cpp.**

6. **The ceiling on one 3090 is real.** Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound at **936 GB/s** (RTX 3090: 24 GB GDDR6X, 384-bit bus, 19.5 Gbps = 936.2 GB/s); [The FPS Review](https://www.thefpsreview.com/gpu-family/nvidia-geforce-rtx-3090-gpu-family-specifications/) a 4-bit 27B reads ~14 GB of weights per token, giving a batch-1 ceiling near ~65 tok/s (consistent with your 44–87 observed). Batching amortizes weight reads across sequences, so aggregate 2–3x is physically plausible — but MTP already spends that same headroom on draft tokens, which is why they don't multiply. A second 3090 buys true 2-lane parallelism (one lane per card) far more cleanly than it buys single-lane speed.

## Details

### Answers to the 7 primary-source technical questions

**Q1 — Does MTP (`--spec-type draft-mtp`) work concurrently with `-np > 1`?**
Per PR #22673 (am17an, "llama + spec: MTP Support"): "Parallel decoding with MTP is supported, but not fully optimized yet," and "MTP is compatible with Vision input and Tensor/Pipeline Parallelism." [github](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) However, the post-merge TODO list still contains "Improve multi-seq performance of the recurrent memory for `n_rs_seq > 0` (currently the multi-seq states are not contiguous in memory so cannot be batched together)." So MTP + `-np>1` is nominally allowed but the recurrent (gated-delta) state cannot yet be batched across slots, so you get little benefit and possible serialization. Additionally, issue #23322 documents that hybrid/SWA models suffer KV-cache invalidation that drops MTP acceptance to ~35–37% with full-prompt re-processing. [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/23322) **Practical conclusion: treat MTP and `-np>1` as mutually exclusive in practice and A/B them; do not assume they stack.**

**Q2 — Can a separate `--model-draft` be combined with MTP?**
The speculative docs allow mixing "an implementation with draft model … with an implementation without draft model," and you can pass multiple `--spec-type` values (e.g., `draft-mtp` + `ngram-mod`). But an external draft model + MTP is redundant, and on your hardware the external draft model is a documented **regression** (see Key Finding 4). VRAM: a 0.6–0.8B draft in Q4/Q8 adds ~0.5–1 GB plus its own KV. Acceptance from PR #22673's own bench: Qwen3.5-0.8B as draft at n_max=16 gave aggregate ~0.68 acceptance [github](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) and did not beat MTP on decode. **Recommendation: MTP only (single stream), no external draft.** For repetitive code refactors, `--spec-type ngram-mod` (a shared-pool n-gram hasher, ~16 MB, the one speculative path that shares state across server slots) is worth A/B-ing under `-np>1`.

**Q3 — `-b`/`-ub` defaults and prefill tuning on Ampere.**
Current defaults (confirmed in `common/common.h` and `tools/server/README.md`): `n_batch = 2048`, `n_ubatch = 512`, `n_parallel = 1` [Joelfernandes](https://www.joelfernandes.org/blog/2026-08-14/ubatch-continuous-batching.html) ("int32_t n_batch = 2048; // logical max batch / int32_t n_ubatch = 512; // physical max batch"). Prefill is compute-bound and reaches the `-ub` ceiling; raising the micro-batch materially speeds prefill. On dual-3090 tuning, `-b 8192 -ub 4096` delivered +77% average prefill throughput at 95K context (burst +309%); [My blog](https://sanj.dev/post/qwen-3-6-27b-dual-rtx-3090-llama-cpp-tuning/) a dedicated RFC (#23262) reports "up to 2x." [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23262) Caveat: larger `-ub` raises peak VRAM during prefill, [Carteakey](https://carteakey.dev/blog/local-inference/local-llm-optimization/) and **MTP negatively impacts prefill** — PR #22673 measured on RTX 3090 that "Prefill is slower with MTP enabled in this PR path: 665 tok/s vs 1315 tok/s on the same GGUF (~0.51x)," [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) and "MTP adds about 2.49 GiB loaded VRAM in this setup." [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) **Recommendation: sweep `-ub` at 768/1024/2048 with `-b` matched, on your real 100K+ prompt shape, watching for OOM.**

**Q4 — KV quant quality vs size, and flash-attention interaction.**
Byte cost scales ~4:2:1 for f16:q8_0:q4_0. Measured decode impact at long context (Discussion #20969, above): ~6K parity, ~24K `q4_0` −11.9%, ~110K `q4_0` **−36.8%** vs f16; prefill unaffected across all three. Quality: literature shows Q4 KV is near-lossless on standard GQA models (<0.1 PPL, per KIVI/KVQuant), but "Evaluating Quantized LLMs" (arXiv 2402.18158) finds long-context (≥4K) tasks are *more* sensitive to KV quant than short ones — and reasoning/coding is exactly where errors compound. Because your model holds KV on only 16/65 layers, moving to `q8_0` (or `f16`) both **speeds long-context decode ~30%** and **removes a quality risk**. Flash-attention (`-fa on`) is required for quantized KV and works cleanly with q8_0/q4_0; note `--split-mode tensor` is incompatible with KV quantization (needs f16). [GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multi-gpu.md) **Recommendation: move K and V to `q8_0` first; test `f16` if VRAM allows at your target per-slot context.**

**Q5 — Newer llama.cpp builds since June 2026.**
Relevant items landed after your `b1-00139b6` build: a **GDN normalization correctness fix** ("Fixes GDN normalization to use rsqrt instead of max for gated delta net q/k, matching flash-linear-attention spec") [Freedom](https://freedom.tech/project/llama-cpp/) affecting all Gated-Delta-Net models (~b10829, Sept 2026); Qwen3.8-Flash-Next support and per-slot context limits; ongoing MTP kernel work (Metal drafting #23114) and hybrid cache fixes. The `ik_llama.cpp` fork ships "fused delta-net (GDN)," MTP, DFlash and tensor-parallel and claims better performance. [GitHub](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/) **Recommendation: upgrade to a current mainline build to pick up the GDN correctness fix and cache-checkpoint fixes, but treat any version bump as output-changing → A/B gate.** Note the checkpoint flag changed: `--checkpoint-every-n-tokens/--ctx-checkpoints` were removed (PR #22929, merged 2026-05-25) and replaced by `-cms, --checkpoint-min-step` (default 8192); checkpoints are now created at user-message boundaries (PR #24176) and evicted if within min-step of an earlier one (PR #25472) [Particula Tech](https://particula.tech/blog/prompt-reprocessing-swa-hybrid-models-kv-cache) — set `-cms` near your median turn length or they get evicted.

**Q6 — Alternative servers (vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT-LLM) on one 3090.**
(a) **Architecture support:** vLLM (≥0.17, native GDN) [Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/deploy-qwen-3-5-gpu-cloud/) and SGLang (via the Qwen3-VL path) both support Qwen3.5/3.8 hybrid GDN. (b) **Fit/context on 24 GB:** the dense 27B AWQ-INT4 loads (~18.8 GiB weights) but leaves only ~2.1 GiB KV → **~64K context ceiling** with fp8 KV on a 24 GB Ampere card (dkhokhlov A10 datapoint: 18.83 GiB weights + ~2.15 GiB KV → 64,744 tokens, [github](https://github.com/dkhokhlov/Qwen_3_6_on_A10_vLLM) single-stream 21.9→19.6 tok/s at 2K→64K; note that the cyankiwi group-32 AWQ *OOMs at construction* — 22.4 > 22.06 GiB param-bound [GitHub](https://github.com/dkhokhlov/Qwen_3_6_on_A10_vLLM) — but the properly-built QuantTrio group-128 AWQ fits). 100K+ on one card needs out-of-tree TurboQuant 3-bit KV + MTP patches (one Medium write-up: ~85 tps @ 125K, single-stream, out-of-tree patches). (c) **MTP/spec + batching:** vLLM supports `qwen3_5_mtp`, but open bug #55533 shows the scheduler runs only ~3 concurrent sequences at batch≥4 for hybrid-GDN+MTP (MTP becomes 2–3x *slower*; measured bs=8: no-spec 367 vs MTP 197 tok/s), and prefix-cache recompute penalties (#53670: a 1,648-token recompute per prefix hit → 30–40% loss; fixed only via opt-in `disable_eagle_block_drop`, not default). [github](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/53670) (d) **Ampere has no FP8** (sm_86); FP8 checkpoints dequantize to FP16, so AWQ/GPTQ-INT4 (Marlin) is the only real vLLM win, and it doesn't help enough. The famous "vLLM scales aggregate 3.9–5.4x c1→c8" figure (dev.to/sikamikanikobg, single RTX 3090) was measured on Gemma-3-1B and ~3–4B-active MoE models that fit with spare VRAM — **not** a dense 27B hybrid. **Verdict: keep llama.cpp.** No published vLLM/SGLang single-3090 number matches your architecture + quant + 100K+ context; the only matched single-card long-context datapoints are llama.cpp's.

**Q7 — Continuous-batching aggregate gain on a bandwidth-bound 3090.**
Theory: at batch 1 decode is memory-bandwidth-bound (weights re-read per token); batching N sequences amortizes that read, so aggregate can rise toward Nx until compute saturates. Measured llama.cpp behavior on a GB10/3090-class card without MTP (NVIDIA forum, blainesworld): aggregate token-gen scaled c1→c2→c4 roughly 13→24→41 tok/s at d0 [nvidia](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/mtp-llama-cpp-a-look-at-qwen3-6-27b/370298) (short context), i.e., ~1.8x at c2 and ~3.1x at c4; the same non-MTP path *rose* with concurrency while MTP stayed flat. A cross-engine 3090 test (dev.to/sikamikanikobg) put llama.cpp continuous-batching scaling at 1.2–1.9x c1→c8 (vs vLLM 3.9–5.4x on MoE/small models). [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sikamikanikobg/vllm-vs-llamacpp-vs-ollama-what-happens-when-your-model-doesnt-fit-in-24gb-vram-56eb) **Realistic expectation for your case: `-np 2` gives ~1.5–1.9x aggregate, `-np 3` ~2–2.5x, likely at the low end at long/filled context — and this exact cell was never measured. It is your #1 experiment.**

### The two levers you already know, re-contextualized
- **MTP `--spec-draft-n-max`:** PR #22673 measured aggregate acceptance ~0.72 at n=3 and ~0.83 at n=2 [github](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) on Qwen3.6-27B, with per-3090 decode gains of ~1.85–1.90x (22.97→42.45 tok/s on the same Q6 GGUF at a 6,978-token prompt). Acceptance falls at longer/filled contexts and for hybrid/SWA models (#23322). Your observation that n=3 gives ~2.0–2.06x but acceptance falls to ~83.5% at 67K is consistent. Going to n=4/n=5 is unlikely to win at 150K–260K: acceptance keeps dropping and rejected drafts still cost verify work; community consensus is n=2–3 for dense Qwen3, with `--spec-draft-p-min 0.75` to gate low-confidence drafts. **Test n=2 vs n=3 with `--spec-draft-p-min`; do not chase n=4/5 at long context.**
- **Parallel slots `-np`:** VRAM cost is tiny (your numbers: +0.02 GiB at np2, +0.26 GiB at np4). The win only materializes under *actual concurrency* with continuous batching; a single request sees nothing. Because MTP doesn't batch across slots, the choice is MTP-single vs slots-many.

### Exploratory levers beyond MTP + slots (accuracy-tagged)
- **Reduce output tokens (biggest win):** `reasoning_effort: medium` via `chat_template_kwargs` (requires `--jinja`, which you already run; Ollama's generic template strips this setting, llama-server keeps it). ~1.8x fewer decode tokens. **Accuracy risk: MEDIUM — reasoning-heavy debugging may regress; the vendor warns lower effort can mean "weaker analysis, more failed actions, and more retries." A/B on the verify oracle before committing.**
- **Prefix caching / KV reuse:** you already run `--cache-reuse 256` ("min chunk size to attempt reusing from the cache via KV shifting," off by default at 0). Maximize hits by keeping the stable system prompt + context file **contiguous and byte-stable at the front** (no timestamps/UUIDs), and pin them with Hermes `protect_first_n`. Each Hermes compaction rotates the session and busts the prefix → re-prefill; on hybrid/SWA models cache invalidation is especially expensive. **Accuracy risk: NONE (prefill-only).**
- **Reduce input tokens per tool call:** Hermes `file_read_max_chars` default 100,000 (~25–35K tokens) — reads exceeding it are rejected with a prompt to use `offset`/`limit`, so *lowering* it forces ranged reads instead of whole-file reads; cap `tool_output.max_bytes` (default 50,000) for pytest/grep dumps. Hermes already deduplicates unchanged re-reads with a lightweight stub (reset on compression). [Nousresearch](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/configuration) **Accuracy risk: LOW — over-aggressive truncation can hide needed context; keep symbol/range reads generous.**
- **Compaction tuning:** `compression.threshold` default 0.50 (with a documented derivation `threshold_tokens = context_length × 0.50`, `tail_token_budget = threshold × target_ratio 0.20`, `max_summary_tokens = min(context_length × 0.05, 12,000)`). [Hermesagent](https://hermesagent.org.cn/en/docs/developer-guide/context-compression-and-caching) Fewer compactions = fewer cache-busting re-prefills but larger resident context (slower per-token); verify how Hermes resolves the model window (262K) so it doesn't compact against the wrong number. The gateway safety valve fires at 85% [Mem0](https://mem0.ai/blog/how-hermes-and-claude-handle-context-compression-in-real-production-agents-%28and-what-you-should-extract%29) and `hygiene_hard_message_limit` (default 400–5000 depending on doc version) is a count-based floor. **Accuracy risk: MEDIUM — lossy summarization can drop hard constraints/exact values; `protect_last_n`/`protect_first_n` mitigate.**
- **Parallel/batched tool calls:** if Hermes issues tool calls in parallel within a turn, overlap the 50–680s pytest runs. **Accuracy risk: NONE (execution-only).**
- **Speculative advances (EAGLE-3/DFlash/DSpark):** llama.cpp supports `draft-eagle3`, `draft-dflash`, `draft-dspark` with Qwen3 backbones, but these need a *separately trained* draft checkpoint for your exact target and add VRAM; no Qwen3.8-27B EAGLE-3/DFlash GGUF is confirmed available. **Lower priority than MTP; revisit if a matched draft ships.**
- **Quantization/kernels:** on Ampere (no FP8), your 4-bit GGUF is already near-optimal for decode bandwidth; the `ik_llama.cpp` fused-GDN kernels are the main "faster at equal quality" candidate to A/B.
- **Ampere-specific:** ensure CUDA graphs are active, set a persistent power/clock (`nvidia-smi -pm 1`, raise power cap so prefill bursts don't throttle), and confirm the model is fully resident (`-ngl 99`, no offload). **Accuracy risk: NONE.**

## Recommendations (ranked, one variable at a time, cheapest measurement first)

**Order of application — apply and A/B each against the verify-lane oracle before the next:**

1. **`reasoning_effort: xhigh → medium`** (Hermes `chat_template_kwargs`). Expected: ~1.8x fewer output tokens → build-lane decode from ~2.4–4.4h to ~1.4–2.5h. Lanes-in-flight: unchanged. **Accuracy risk: MEDIUM — gate on oracle.** *Threshold to keep: verify-lane pass rate and mutant-kill/test-gate parity within noise vs xhigh baseline.*
2. **KV cache `q4_0 → q8_0`** (`-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0`). Expected: +~30% long-context decode AND higher quality. Lanes: unchanged. **Accuracy risk: NONE/positive.** *Threshold: confirm it fits at your per-slot context; if VRAM-tight, `f16` is affordable because only 16/65 layers hold KV.*
3. **Hermes input trims:** lower `file_read_max_chars` (force ranged reads), cap `tool_output.max_bytes`. Expected: cut the 4.5–5.7M input tokens → less prefill (1.3–2.1h → ~0.8–1.5h). **Accuracy risk: LOW.**
4. **Run the concurrency matrix and pick the throughput mode** (the pivotal experiment): compare **MTP `-np 1`** vs **non-MTP `-np 2` and `-np 3`** on real 100K+ lane-shaped requests. Adopt whichever maximizes *aggregate* throughput for 2 concurrent lanes. Expected: 2 lanes concurrent at ~2.5–3.5h each (non-MTP `-np 2`), or serialized ~1.9x single-lane (MTP). **Accuracy risk: NONE (both are lossless).**
5. **`-ub` prefill sweep** (768/1024/2048) once the decode mode is fixed. Expected: faster prefill, especially non-MTP. **Accuracy risk: NONE.**
6. **Upgrade llama.cpp** to a current build (GDN rsqrt fix, cache-checkpoint fixes, `-cms`). **Accuracy risk: LOW — A/B, then keep if oracle parity holds.**
7. **Prefix-stability + `protect_first_n`** to maximize `--cache-reuse` hits across 100–250 tool calls. **Accuracy risk: NONE.**

**Headline projection on the recommended config (steps 1–5, non-MTP `-np 2`, q8_0 KV, reasoning medium):** build lane **~2.5–3.5h** with **2 lanes concurrent**; verify lane **~1–2h**. If the concurrency matrix shows non-MTP `-np 2` aggregate < ~1.5x, fall back to **MTP `-np 1`** and run lanes serially through OmniRoute — build lane ~2.5–3.5h single, lanes serialized.

**OmniRoute:** set its in-flight admission to match your chosen `-np` (2 for the concurrent config) so both lanes map to distinct slots without the 60s admission queue stalling the second lane.

## The CEILING statement (honest)

**One 3090 realistically tops out around ~2–2.5x aggregate speedup on the inference itself**, because decode is memory-bandwidth-bound at 936 GB/s and the two throughput levers (MTP token-batching and multi-slot sequence-batching) draw from the *same* bandwidth-amortization budget and therefore do not multiply. You cannot get MTP's ~1.9x *and* continuous batching's ~1.9x at once on this card. Your path to the 2–3h / 2-lane target is therefore **mostly won on the workload side** (fewer output tokens via reasoning_effort, fewer input tokens via Hermes, and removing the q4_0 KV decode tax) rather than on raw inference multipliers.

**A second GPU changes the shape of the problem more than the speed of one lane.** Two 3090s give only ~1.5x single-request speed over PCIe (no NVLink), or somewhat more aggregate with an NVLink bridge halving the tensor-parallel sync penalty — but the clean win is **capacity**: run **one llama-server + one full-context MTP lane per card**, giving **two truly independent lanes** each at full MTP single-stream speed (~1.9x), with no slot contention and no KV budget compromise. That is the most reliable route to "2+ lanes, each 2–3h, equal-or-better accuracy." NVLink helps only if you also want to tensor-split a single lane; for one-lane-per-card it is unnecessary.

## NOT-RESOLVED — measure on the owner's PC (runnable commands + decision rules)

1. **Concurrency matrix (the decisive one).** Run each config and read `/metrics` (`llamacpp:n_busy_slots_per_decode`, prompt/gen throughput) and `/slots` (per-slot speed) under 2–3 concurrent 100K+ lane-shaped requests:
   - MTP single: `llama-server -m Qwen3.8-27B-UD-IQ4_XS.gguf -ngl 99 -c 262144 -fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -np 1 --spec-type draft-mtp --spec-draft-n-max 3 --spec-draft-p-min 0.75 --jinja --metrics`
   - Non-MTP np2: same but `-np 2 -c 262144` (2×131K slots) and **remove** `--spec-type`.
   - Non-MTP np3: `-np 3 -c 196608` (3×~65K slots).
   **Decision rule:** adopt the config with the highest *aggregate* decode tok/s for 2 concurrent lanes; require ≥1.5x aggregate over MTP-single to justify dropping MTP. If < 1.5x, keep MTP `-np 1` and serialize lanes.
2. **KV type fit + speed.** Sweep `-ctk/-ctv` ∈ {q4_0, q8_0, f16} at your real per-slot context; measure decode tok/s at 100K/200K and VRAM headroom. **Rule:** pick the highest-precision KV that fits with ≥1 GiB VRAM headroom; require q8_0 decode ≥ q4_0 at 110K (expected ~+30%).
3. **`-ub` prefill sweep:** `llama-bench -m ... -p 4096 -n 128 -b 4096 -ub 768,1024,2048 -ngl 99 -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -fa on`, then confirm the winner inside llama-server. **Rule:** keep the `-ub` with best pp t/s that does not OOM at your max prompt.
4. **reasoning_effort A/B:** run the full verify lane at `xhigh` vs `medium` on the same tasks. **Rule:** keep `medium` iff items-closed / red→green / mutant-kill / discrepancy gates are within noise of `xhigh`.

## Caveats
- The **aggregate throughput of 2–4 concurrent lane-shaped requests was never measured** on your box; every parallel-lane projection here is theory-backed but must be confirmed by experiment #1.
- Public MTP/throughput numbers are largely on **Qwen3.6-27B** and on **DGX Spark/GB10 or dual-3090**, not your exact single-3090 + Qwen3.8-27B + IQ4_XS pairing; use them as directional, not literal.
- The `q4_0` KV decode-penalty figure (−36.8% at 110K) comes from a corrected DGX Spark GB10 benchmark on a 30B-A3B MoE (Discussion #20969); the magnitude on your card/context should be re-measured, but the direction is robust and independently reasoned (per-token dequant overhead scales with KV length).
- Any lever that changes model outputs (reasoning_effort, KV type, engine/build upgrade, MTP settings) **must pass the verify-lane oracle** before adoption; a faster-but-worse lane is rejected by definition.
- Some 2026-dated community sources (blogs, enthusiast forks, single-run forum posts) are unaudited; treat their exact tok/s as indicative rather than authoritative, and note the vLLM issue threads are self-disclosed as AI-assisted.

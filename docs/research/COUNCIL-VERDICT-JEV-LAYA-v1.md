### Acceptable Compromises

1. **Haiku's outputs as bootstrap labels** for B1/B2, accepted with Karpathy's caveat stated in the artifact: this fits confidence to *imitation*, not correctness (transcript, Karpathy R1). The compensating control is a blind, human-audited sample as the promotion bar — never Haiku-agreement alone.
2. **One week of calendar delay** for the shadow label clock, accepted in exchange for not building serving plumbing ahead of a demonstrated capability. Rams and Meadows judged the delay cheap; Taleb reversed his Round-1 sequencing to agree.
3. **FP32 only.** No INT8/OpenVINO effort on Zen 3 — the "Is INT8 Portable?" result (part 1 §Q3: cores without a dot-product ISA "slow down by 1.7x") makes it a negative-expected-value experiment until VNNI hardware exists.
4. **D1's labels are the coordinator's own ratings.** Capturing them is circular (the model would be calibrated against the judge it is meant to relieve). Accepted for corpus-building only; the circularity is declared in the ledger's provenance field and forbids D1 as an inference point (see Rejections).
5. **A new pinned upstream that may be discarded.** The Laya checkpoint and the `laya` PyPI version must enter `upstream.lock.yaml` (rule 13) before the first probe, even though the probes may kill the layer.
6. **A fail-open marker** (`[sieve: fail-open]`) on any future judge failure, accepted over fail-closed, because a fail-closed advisory layer would become a production dependency — the outcome rule 12 forbids.
7. **The 46.9 % / 90 % byte-share wins stay unclaimed.** They are the findings' own first-order projections from our stores (brief §1; part 1 TL;DR), not measurements. No status line may cite them as achieved.

### Kill Criteria

Each is observable without re-convening the council, tied to a threshold or event, and windowed.

- **KC-J1 — gate reachability (absorbing barrier, no threshold to retune).** If `scripts/no_laya_in_gates.py` reports any Laya/System-One vocabulary (`laya`, `systemone`, `system_one`, `decide ask`, `sieve`, `jev`) inside a gate-defining file, or a Laya value is shown reachable from a gate/merge/lint/commit/push/proof predicate, at any time after the screen lands (due 2026-10-06) — the verdict is invalidated and we pull the layer, revert the integration, and regenerate every dependent attested artifact (AF-AP-56 already proved a tooling change invalidates minted results). Unanimous; Taleb's non-negotiable, Meadows' mechanism.
- **KC-J1b — gate-adjacent flow (Chairman's extension, evidence-based).** A Laya score must never be the sole evidence for an ANTI-PATTERN REGISTRY row, which mechanically extends the deterministic `AP_SCREEN` (`docs/research/findings/jev-audit/our-decision-points-evidence.md` row 9), nor for a verifier per-finding class, whose mutant set becomes the next repair brief's acceptance bar (same file, rows 1–3). If such a row or class is ever traced to a Laya score with no human step in between, by any date, that point reverts to shadow and the flow is repaired before the next commit.
- **KC-J2 — latency.** If FP32 p50 > 300 ms/question at 4 threads on **both** venues, measured and filed by 2026-10-06, the verdict's sync class is invalidated: reject C1, C2, B6 and F2 permanently and continue only as an offline/batch sieve on the PC. If p50 > 1,500 ms/question on the PC at 6 threads by the same date, stop the layer entirely — at the repo's own ~40,000 tool-results/day scale (part 1 §Build spec, Ledger) the judging cost cannot be hidden. Threshold source: part 1 §Q3 decision rule. DEALBREAKER for Torvalds and Meadows.
- **KC-J3 — signal.** If, on ≥100 blind hand-labeled B2 hit-role cases filed by 2026-10-20, Laya's post-temperature argmax accuracy is ≤ max(majority-class baseline, regex/heuristic baseline), B2 is rejected. If the same holds for B1 by 2026-11-03, the whole classification tier is rejected and only the ledger survives, as a corpus for a possible future fine-tune. Karpathy's non-negotiable; it is the first-ever test of the rejection checklist's first clause against Laya's actual output.
- **KC-J4 — calibration.** If per-question-type ECE, on ≥200 labels with equal-frequency bins B = 10–15 plus an ECEsweep cross-check, exceeds **0.05** for any point whose score a human reads, or **0.10** for a reorder-only point, after temperature fitting, by 2026-11-17 — that point stays shadow-only forever. (Threshold conflict resolved below; the 0.05 figure is the brief's §4.2, the 0.10 is part 2 Rec 2.)
- **KC-J5 — needle loss (absorbing barrier).** If **one** incident occurs in which a dropped or hidden item changed a decision, at any date, drop mode is off permanently and everywhere; the layer is reorder/shadow only. Taleb's; not a threshold to retune.
- **KC-J6 — provenance and recoverability.** If any Laya-produced decision is found in any store without all of {producer, checkpoint digest, question id, calibration state, incumbent answer}, or any surface displays a Laya score without the original bytes recoverable adjacent to it, by any date after the first shadow row is written, that point reverts to shadow until repaired. Taleb R2 plus his enforcement-round extension.
- **KC-J7 — label famine.** If the retrospective harvest plus one week of shadow yields fewer than 200 labels for the highest-volume question type by 2026-11-03, the calibration path is unreachable on this project's traffic: stop at the ledger, ship nothing further, and re-open only if decision volume changes. Labels are the binding constraint (Taleb's enforcement-round counterfactual).
- **KC-J8 — attention and compute contention.** If, in any 7-day window before 2026-11-17, a Stage-0 proof lane misses a slot, or PC lane-admission refusals rise above the measured steady-state baseline, and either is attributable to a Laya job, suspend the layer until the remaining Stage-0 proofs mint. Meadows' two-stocks concern, made observable.
- **KC-J9 — egress.** If `strace -f -e trace=connect` or `ss -tanp` shows any non-loopback connect from the decide process on either venue, on the first deploy attempt, fail the deploy and do not retry until the cause is found. Rule 3; part 1 NOT-RESOLVED #8.

### Unresolved Questions

Lead with what the council does not know. Items 1, 3 and 9 are the Chairman's; the rest are the panel's own.

1. **What FP32 actually costs on this hardware — and which of three latency families is real.** Nothing has been measured on either venue (brief §1). Worse, the evidence base carries three unreconciled numbers and no member cited more than one. Part 1 states "the measured ~1.0–1.25 s/question FP32 floor" (TL;DR, and again in Caveats and §Q4) with no located producer. Part 1 §1 also reports the upstream README's "193–464 ms/request on CPU when preloaded". The audit's in-repo evidence (`docs/research/findings/jev-audit/laya-evidence.md:75-77`, marked SOLID) reads, from `research/results/cpu_51_language_sweep.json` at 4 threads: 116–256 ms per one-question case for the English model, and `ms_per_case` 1392.5 for typed-decisions at **five questions per case** — about 278 ms per question. `docs/08_DECISION_LOG.md` D-040 already records the 116–256 ms figure as the project's own understanding. So the plan's design-shaping premise ("inline is infeasible; async/PC-only by default") and the council's 300 ms dealbreaker rest on a number roughly 4× worse than every primary CPU measurement in the evidence base, one of which may simply be a per-case figure read as per-question. The CPU model in that sweep is **not recorded** (the audit marks it UNSURE) and it ran `laya` 0.2.0 on torch 2.8.0, so it does not prove the 5600X clears 300 ms. It does mean the measurement is decisive and cheap, and that the architecture may be less constrained than the findings assume. **Settled by:** the Concrete Next Step.
2. **Whether Laya has any signal on the narrow tasks we would actually ask.** 0.362 is a 20-option aggregate on someone else's benchmark (part 1 §1 correction (a)). B2 is a 5-option question over compact state; B1 is 3 options plus a small kind set. Karpathy named this as where he may be wrong in both directions. Nobody knows. **Settled by:** KC-J3's blind 100.
3. **Whether the calibration target is reachable at all.** The 0.081 fitted-ECE figure the plan leans on is third-party (part 2 §1, cited to AI Weekly/Flowtivity). The repo's **own out-of-sample refit** for typed-decisions lands at **ECE 0.1287** (`laya-evidence.md:75`, SOLID) — above part 2's ≤0.10 promotion bar and 2.6× the brief's 0.05 kill threshold. If our questions calibrate like the vendor's 20-option benchmark, KC-J4 fires and every point is shadow-only forever. Our 2–5 option questions may calibrate far better; that is unmeasured. **Settled by:** the per-question-type ECE run at KC-J4.
4. **Whether either output-transform seam fires.** The Q5 (`PostToolUse updatedToolOutput` on Claude Code 2.1.278, issue #68951) and Q4 (`transform_terminal_output` on the pinned Hermes) fire-tests have not been run (brief §1). Not blocking — the first increment needs no seam — but no integration point may be promised until one fires.
5. **Whether a regex beats Laya on the same held-out set.** The rejection checklist's first clause has never been measured against Laya's output (Karpathy R1). Untested for every candidate point.
6. **The label yield of the retrospective harvest.** Nobody counted. B1/B2 are produced by `hive-reviewer` / `hive-scout` as Haiku returns (our-decision-points-evidence rows 6–7), which land in transcripts and lane reports, not in a durable store. If the harvest yields under 200 per type, "ledger-first" produces nothing on a reasonable clock — KC-J7 exists precisely because the council could not answer this.
7. **Whether D1's self-labels can ever calibrate anything.** The coordinator rates; the model would be calibrated against the rater it is meant to relieve. No member proposed a way out.
8. **The CPU cost of a resident FP32 checkpoint beside four local-route lanes on the PC.** ~1.7 GB resident per checkpoint and a saturating batch-1 matmul (part 1 §Q3, §Q10) against the PC's measured admission ceiling. Unmodelled by anyone.
9. **Whether the owner wants this now at all.** The council assumed the appetite. Attention is the scarcest stock (Meadows R1) and eleven Stage-0 items remain in flight.
10. **Whether the byte-share projections hold.** 46.9 % of coordinator transcript and 90 % of PC-lane context bytes are the findings' projections from our own stores; no per-venue byte floor has been histogrammed (part 1 NOT-RESOLVED #4).

The council's plan is **ledger-first**: measure, capture labels, then decide whether the service is worth building. Four numbered increments; the first two are seedable now.

No service, no hook, no ledger dependency. Precedent for a measurement spike before the seed: Stage 0's spike #0 reclassified two proofs before increment 1.

*Venue:* sandbox. No model, no bridge, no GPU.

*Deterministic acceptance test per step (all LLM-free, each with its negative control):*
1. **Redaction.** A state with an injected secret (`sk-…`, a bridge token, a private-key env value) produces a digest identical to the same state with the secret absent, and the secret's bytes never appear in the ledger. *Negative control:* remove the redactor → the test goes red on the exact assertion.
2. **Provenance.** A row missing any of {producer, checkpoint digest (or the literal `none`), question id, calibration state, incumbent answer} is refused with the exact error `decision-row-incomplete: missing <field>`. *Negative control:* relax the check → red. This is KC-J6's mechanism.
3. **Append-only + replay.** N rows written, then replayed byte-identically; a mutated row is caught by digest mismatch with an exact named error; the whole run twice, bitwise identical.
4. **Harvest.** Over a committed fixture of M archived reviewer/scout returns, exactly M rows with stable digests, run twice bitwise identical; a malformed return is refused by name, never silently dropped. The harvest prints `harvest: <n> rows, <k> per question type` and the increment's report **pastes that line verbatim** — that line is the label-yield measurement KC-J7 reads.
5. **Never-a-gate screen.** Exits 0 on the tree; a fixture gate file carrying `laya`/`systemone` exits non-zero naming file:line; wired into pre-commit. *Negative control:* strip the vocabulary from the fixture → green.
6. **No model dependency.** The whole J1 suite passes with `laya` not installed.

**J2 — the signal probe (file by 2026-10-20).** Draw a blind stratified sample of 100 B2 hit-role cases from the J1 ledger, hand-label them, and score Laya (post-temperature) against three comparators printed side by side: the majority-class baseline, a regex/heuristic baseline, and the Haiku incumbent. *Venue:* PC. *Acceptance:* the sample list is committed by digest before any scoring; the report prints all four numbers and the option count. KC-J3 reads this report.

**J3 — the service, only if J0 and J2 both pass.** Then, and only then, build `laya-decide` per part 1's six-step build spec (model host → warm-start single-worker queue → both endpoints → canonical builder → ledger → two-venue deploy with the no-outbound proof), each step with the acceptance test part 1 already specifies. Then the Q4/Q5 fire-tests; adopt only a seam proven to fire. Then B2 in shadow with full provenance, then B1. Nothing else.

### Consensus & Agreement

**The panel moved during the session, and the move is the most important fact in this transcript.** Rounds 1 and 2 reached consensus on *build now, shadow-only* — all five members endorsed building the loopback service and ledger immediately in a D-029-style parallel lane, disagreeing only about which integration points came along. The enforcement round reversed it to *ledger-first*, unanimously, in Round 3.

**The argument that moved them,** in the order it landed: Taleb's counterfactual — *labels are the binding constraint; build only what produces them* — showed that the ledger alone yields the scarce asset with none of the serving surface, so build-now was buying a dependency before its payoff was measured. Karpathy then removed build-now's last defence: *shadow mode does not rescue this — networks fail silently and confidently, so shadow logs confident wrongness at scale instead of catching it.* Rams restated it in the project's own vocabulary — a component that exists **looks validated**, a hollow green in architecture form. Meadows supplied the structural reason to prefer the reordering: ledger-first changes *when* the reinforcing loop (service exists → integrations accrete → harder to kill) is allowed to start, rather than patching a parameter onto an already-spinning loop. Note that build-now's own best argument survived into the new consensus: Meadows and Rams both showed in Round 2 that *deferring past Stage 0* is an invented serial dependency (D-029's parallel-lane exemption; Laya touches no proof and no spine), so nobody returned to "wait for Stage 0". The panel moved to a third position neither Round-1 camp held.

**Q3 — the rollout gates and their numeric thresholds.** Five gates; the first two are new and are the council's principal addition to the findings' ladder.

- **Gate 0 (probe — no service).** FP32 p50 ≤ 300 ms/question at 4 threads (part 1 §Q3 decision rule), plus bitwise-identical probabilities on a repeated input. Failing it does not necessarily kill the layer; it kills the synchronous class (KC-J2).
- **Gate 1 (signal — the council's addition).** On ≥100 blind hand-labels: post-temperature argmax accuracy **>** both the majority-class baseline and a regex/heuristic baseline on the same set; and calibrated argmax agreement with the Haiku incumbent ≥ the incumbent's own accuracy on that sample (part 2 Rec 2). Karpathy's KC; it is the first real test of the rejection checklist's first clause.
- **Gate 2 (seam).** Adopt only the seam the Q5/Q4 fire-tests prove fires; on Hermes the sieve enforces its own inner deadline and fails open with the `[sieve: fail-open]` marker, because `transform_*` is abandoned, not fail-closed, on timeout, and `pre_tool_call` fails **closed** and is therefore out of bounds (part 1 §Q4).
- **Gate 3 (shadow, one week, both lanes).** Regret ≤ 2 % at the chosen drop threshold on hand labels; ≥ 80 % of bytes above the per-venue floor (part 1 Rec 3). Council additions: 100 % provenance completeness on logged rows (KC-J6) and zero screen hits (KC-J1).
- **Gate 4 (calibrate, then A/B).** Per-question-type temperature on ≥ 200–750 labels, equal-frequency bins B = 10–15 plus an ECEsweep cross-check; needle retention ≥ 98 %; a net token reduction that survives the added latency (part 1 Rec 3–4). **ECE threshold — a conflict between the source documents, resolved here:** the brief (§4.2) sets ECE > 0.05 as a rejection; part 2 Rec 2 sets ≤ ~0.10. *Chairman's tie-break, flagged as such:* 0.05 binds for any point whose score a human reads (Rams' honesty requirement — a number on a screen borrows trust); 0.10 binds for reorder-only points where every item stays recoverable. Both are put in jeopardy by Unresolved #3: the vendor's own out-of-sample refit on 20-option typed-decisions lands at 0.1287. Implication the council should have drawn and did not: **do not attempt a point with more than ~10 options before Stage 4.**
- **Gate 5 (specialize).** Fine-tune only if accuracy is the bottleneck *and* the ledger holds a few thousand labels for that question (~30k questions, 2×T4, ~4–5 h — part 1 Rec 5).
